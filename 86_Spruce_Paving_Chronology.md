# 86 Spruce Street — The Paving of the Parking Lot

**A documented chronology, with sources.**

Subject property: 86 Spruce Street, Princeton, NJ — Block 30.03, Lot 100 (formerly Lots 62 and 63).
Zoning Board file: **Z1616-420** (2017). Zoning permit: **Z19-712** (2019).

Every factual statement below is linked to the document it comes from. Where the record is silent, that is stated as silence and not as inference.

> **Note on one filename.** [`86_Spruce_Street_Site_Plan_2019.pdf`](docs/86_Spruce_Street_Site_Plan_2019.pdf) is named for the year it was obtained, not the year it was drawn. It contains the eight-sheet Kensho Resources LLC set **dated November 14, 2016**, revised through April 5, 2017 and sealed by James J. Chmielak, P.E. That is the plan set the Zoning Board approved, and the plan set the 2019 permit application names as its authority.

## 2016–2017: The variance to keep the lot gravel

**November 14, 2016 — the site plan shows the lot remaining gravel.**
Kensho Resources LLC prepares an eight-sheet set for Block 30.03, Lots 62 & 63. Sheet 3, the Minor Site Plan, carries the printed annotation **"EXIST. GRAVEL PARKING LOT (TO REMAIN),"** alongside "EXIST. GRAVEL DRIVEWAY (TO REMAIN)" and "EXIST. CONC. SIDEWALK (TO REMAIN)."
→ [Kensho site plan set, 11/14/16 rev. 4/5/17](docs/86_Spruce_Street_Site_Plan_2019.pdf)

**December 6, 2016 — the application is deemed incomplete.**
Land Use Engineer John M. West, P.E. writes to Chris Tarr, Esq. of Stevens & Lee: former Borough Code § 17A-390 requires parking lots to be paved and landscaped, so "the plans should be revised **or a variance requested**." This is ordinary process, not a contradiction — it is the letter that sends the applicant to the Board.
→ [Incompleteness letter, 12/6/16](docs/Spruce_Street_-_86_Application_Incomplete.pdf)

**January 30, 2017 — landscape review.**
Daniel Dobromilsky, P.L.A. reviews the landscape plan for the municipality: 26 tagged trees, 48 Green Giant Arborvitae proposed around the lot perimeter as a buffer, concerns about declining and invasive specimens.
→ [Dobromilsky memo, 1/30/17](docs/dobromilsky-86_Spruce_St_1-1-30-17.pdf)

**January 31, 2017 — the joint staff report recommending gravel.**
West and Zoning Officer Derek W. Bridger jointly report to the Zoning Board. Under *Bulk Variance*:

> "Staff is in support of the variance as paving the lot would increase the rate of runoff over the lot and have a negative effect on the adjoining property to the north."

Under *Surface Water Drainage*, staff note the lot appears to have been expanded roughly 20 feet northward since 1989–90 and recommend **installation of a rain garden** to compensate.
→ [West/Bridger Engineering and Zoning Report, 1/31/17](docs/Spruce_Street_-_86_ZBA_memo.pdf)
→ [Completeness and hearing-date letter, 1/31/17](docs/Spruce_Street_-_86_completion_and_meeting_info_ltr.pdf)
→ [Certified 200-foot owners list, 1/31/17](docs/Spruce_Street_-_86_property_owners_list.pdf)

**February 10, 2017 — the neighborhood is told the lot will not be paved.**
Certified mail to every owner within 200 feet, including Frank J. & Vivian E. Ryle (80 Spruce), Steven & Karla Schultz (82 Spruce), and Patricia DiMaio (96 Spruce). The enclosed letter from 86 Spruce Street, LLC states:

> "We are seeking permission **not to pave** the gravel parking lot, in an effort to minimize runoff... The town staff supports keeping it gravel."

→ [Noticing package — affidavit of mailing, published notice, neighbor letter, certified receipts](docs/Spruce_Street_-_86_noticing_docs.pdf)

**February 22, 2017 — the Board grants the variance. Memorialized March 22, 2017.**
Three things happen in one resolution, and they are distinct:

1. A **c(2) variance** to permit a non-paved parking area in exception to § 17A-390(b), on the express finding that it "will promote environmental conservation because a paved lot would increase the rate of runoff over the Property and negatively impact the adjoining property to the north."
2. **Minor site plan approval** "as set forth in the application and **the project plans as presented**" — the plans that say the lot remains gravel.
3. **Six conditions**, including **Condition 4**: "The applicant shall insure that the proposed development shall not cause increased runoff onto neighboring properties."

Chmielak testified the applicant would *reduce* impervious coverage by 500 SF. Richard Scott, property manager and husband of the LLC's sole member, testified he had observed no runoff to adjacent properties.
→ [Findings of Fact, Conclusions and Resolution, Z1616-420](docs/Spruce_Street_86__resolution.pdf)
→ [Affidavit of publication, 4/7/17](docs/Spruce_Street_86__resolution_affidavit_of_publication.pdf)

**March 21 and April 6, 2017 — compliance conditions.**
The rain garden must be elongated along the entire width of the parking lot, with a planting list and maintenance schedule. A second memo adds the consolidation of Lots 62 and 63.
→ [Compliance memo, 3/21/17](docs/Spruce_Street_-_86_Compliance_Memo.pdf)
→ [Compliance memo, 4/6/17](docs/Spruce_Street_-_86_Compliance_Memo_4-6-17.pdf)

**April 4, 2017 — the bond.**
Construction estimate $20,653; performance guarantee $24,783.60. Line items include **"Rain Garden Installation, 1 LS, $3,500"** and 32 LF of 8″ HDPE pipe. The rain garden is a funded, bonded obligation as of April 2017.
→ [Bond estimate letter, 4/4/17](docs/Spruce_Street_-_86_bond_ltr.pdf)

**May 8, 2017 — the plans are certified compliant, and the property changes hands the same day.**
West to Bridger: the plans as revised April 5, 2017 are "in compliance with the findings of fact," no objection to signing. On the same date the deed to Barsky Enterprises, LLC is delivered for $1,600,000.
→ [Sign plans memo, 5/8/17](docs/Spruce_Street_-_86_Sign_plans_memo.pdf)
→ [Deed and closing documents](docs/doc00903420170508180206.pdf)

> **On the parties.** The applicant throughout 2016–17 was **86 Spruce Street, LLC**, whose sole member was Rajka B.V. Scott. **Barsky Enterprises, LLC** was the buyer, not the applicant. The deed was executed August 23, 2016 and delivered May 8, 2017, and Barsky recorded a **Notice of Settlement on September 22, 2016** — before the site plan application was filed — renewed April 4 and May 5, 2017. Barsky took title subject to the resolution and its conditions.

## 2019: The lot is paved

**October 21, 2019 — application.**
Owner listed as "86 Spruce, LLC" (the seller's entity, two and a half years after title passed to Barsky Enterprises). Contact: Daniel Barsky. Under *Prior Approvals Required*, the Site Plan Approval box is checked with the handwritten entry: **"Pave Driveway per site plan attached dated 11/14/16."** No box is checked in the *Development Type & Fees* section.
→ [Zoning permit file Z19-712](docs/86_Spruce_Zoning_Permit_R.pdf)

**October 24, 2019 — the permit is refused.**
Bridger to 86 Spruce Street, LLC, attn. Daniel Barsky. The stated deficiency is not that paving conflicts with a Board condition. It is a request for paving plans showing the driveway and planting island "**as discussed with Jack West, P.E.**" — evidence of a conversation that appears nowhere in the file.
→ [Refusal of Permit, 10/24/19](docs/86_Spruce_Zoning_Permit_R.pdf)

**October 29, 2019 — something is resubmitted.** Date-stamped received. Not produced.

**October 30, 2019 — permit issued. Fee $60.00.**
The stamped drawing is a cropped sheet showing a driveway corridor, two garage bays and a porch, hand-annotated "DRIVEWAY," "GARAGE," "GARAGE," "Replacement tree," and a note that substitute plantings for three arborvitae will go in the rain garden. It states "TOTAL AREA OF DISTURB. 4,400 SF (0.10 AC.)." At the bottom margin, two lines of rotated text are sliced by the page edge, legible only as "...VEL" over "...G LOT" — the screened area label **GRAVEL PARKING LOT**.

The garage bays appear on the 92-94 Spruce (Lot 64) plan set, not on the 86 Spruce set, which depicts that structure only as an existing house. **Nothing on the stamped drawing depicts paving the parking lot.**
→ [Stamped sheet, 10/30/19](docs/86_Spruce_Zoning_Permit_R.pdf) · [92-94 Spruce plan set](docs/92_Spruce_Street_Site_Plan_2019.pdf) · [86 Spruce plan set](docs/86_Spruce_Street_Site_Plan_2019.pdf)

**November 17–19, 2019 — neighbors object; the engineer responds.**
Vivian Ryle writes about "potential flooding at the rear of our property due to the impending paving of the parking lot." West replies the next morning:

> "As you are aware there has always been a large gravel parking lot at 86 Spruce, **with no stormwater management**, the Princeton stormwater ordinance considers gravel to be 100% impervious, and therefore the developer is **allowed** to pave. To be conservative **we required** the developer to install a rain garden with an overflow that ties into the municipal system."

Three things about that sentence. It is the opposite of the engineering position in the January 31, 2017 report. The rain garden was not a 2019 requirement — it was a 2017 condition, bonded at $3,500. And the statement that there had been no stormwater management is either wrong, or means a 2017 condition went unbuilt for two years while the municipality held the guarantee.

Steven Schultz replies the same day that the gravel has in fact been functioning as a permeable surface, with no erosion or silt at the bottom of the slope, and that asphalt will increase both volume and velocity. West later writes: "I met with the developer yesterday and he has agreed to make all the corrections outlined in your email." That meeting does not appear in the file.

Note that West wrote **"allowed to pave,"** not required. Schultz's December 13 message says "we had no choice in adding all that asphalt," which most naturally reads as the neighbors having no say rather than the developer being compelled. Nothing in the record establishes that anyone was told paving was mandatory.
→ [Email chain, 11/17/19 – 12/13/19](docs/doc00075820260828125515_R.pdf)

**July 13, 2022 — the guarantee is released.**
James J. Purcell, P.E. to Administrator Bernard Hvozdovic: "All maintenance work has been inspected and found to be satisfactory." No inspection report, date, inspector or photograph accompanies it. Escrow Nos. 17-465 and 17-466.
→ [Release of maintenance guarantee, 7/13/22](docs/Spruce_Street_86__release_of_maint.pdf)

## Unexplained in the record

Seven things are missing. For each, the source that would ordinarily require it.

### 1. No revised site plan showing paving

The Board approved minor site plan approval of "the project plans as presented." Site plan review and approval is governed by **N.J.S.A. 40:55D-46** (site plan) and **N.J.S.A. 40:55D-46.1** (minor site plan). An approval attaches to the plan approved; work materially different from it is not covered by that approval. Changing the surface of the parking area from gravel — the feature the plans label "TO REMAIN" — is a change to the approved plan.

*What would explain it:* an amended site plan application, or a determination that the change was de minimis. Neither is in the file.

### 2. No Board action amending the 2017 approval

The relief granted was a **c(2) variance under N.J.S.A. 40:55D-70(c)**, together with conditions imposed under the Board's authority. Variances and conditions are the Board's to grant and the Board's to modify. A zoning officer has no power to grant relief from, or vary, a condition of a board resolution — the zoning officer's role under **N.J.S.A. 40:55D-18** is enforcement, and a party aggrieved by a zoning officer's determination appeals to the Board under **N.J.S.A. 40:55D-70(a)**.

*What would explain it:* an application to the Zoning Board to amend Z1616-420, and a resolution acting on it. Neither is in the file.

### 3. No notice to the neighbors

Notice is not triggered by area or by cost. It is triggered by an **application to a board**: **N.J.S.A. 40:55D-12** (who must be noticed, including owners within 200 feet) and **N.J.S.A. 40:55D-11** (contents of notice). The absence of notice here is therefore a *consequence* of item 2 — no board application was filed, so no notice was owed.

The contrast is still the point. The neighborhood was noticed by certified mail in February 2017 about a proposal **not to pave**, and noticed later about tree removal at 92-94 Spruce. It was never noticed about the paving itself.

### 4. No permit for paving the parking lot

The permit that issued authorizes what its drawing depicts. The application says "Pave Driveway"; the stamped drawing shows a driveway corridor and garage bays; the parking lot appears only as a label sliced at the page margin.

*What would explain it:* a drawing in the Z19-712 file depicting or dimensioning work to the parking area, or a second permit. Neither is in the production. Both are requested in the supplemental OPRA.

### 5. No engineering approval under § 10B-246.1

The municipality's own permit application form states, in print: **"Addition of 400 sq. ft. or greater requires Engineering Approval 10B-246.1."** The stamped drawing states 4,400 square feet of disturbance — eleven times that threshold — and the parking lot is several times larger again.

*What would explain it:* an approval, review or calculation under § 10B-246.1. Nothing responsive was produced.

### 6. No stormwater review

The same form states: **"Applications will be subject to review for Road Opening/Right-of-way Occupancy, Tree Removal, and Stormwater Management, as required by Code of Princeton."** Beyond the local code, the state **Stormwater Management Rules, N.J.A.C. 7:8**, treat creation of regulated motor vehicle surface as a threshold question in its own right, not merely a net-increase question. Separately, land disturbance of **5,000 square feet or more** requires certification by the Mercer County Soil Conservation District under the **Soil Erosion and Sediment Control Act, N.J.S.A. 4:24-39 et seq.**, and **N.J.A.C. 2:90**. The stamped figure of 4,400 SF sits just below that threshold; the actual disturbance, if it included the parking lot, did not.

*What would explain it:* a stormwater review, calculation or determination; or a soil conservation district certification. Neither is in the production.

### 7. No reference anywhere in 2019 to the 2017 record

Not in the application. Not in the refusal letter. Not on the stamped drawing. Not in West's emails to the neighbors. Not in the 2022 release. The variance, the resolution, the site plan approval and Condition 4 are absent from every 2019 and 2022 document in the file.

*What would explain it:* a memorandum, determination or opinion addressing whether the 2017 conditions remained binding. Nothing responsive was produced.

## Open questions

- **When was the rain garden actually built?** Bonded April 2017. Existing but "inadequate" by November 2019. Guarantee released July 2022. If it was not installed until 2019, a funded 2017 condition went unbuilt for two years while the municipality held the guarantee — and what West described as a conservative new requirement was an overdue obligation.
- **What was submitted on October 29, 2019?** The refusal demanded a resubmission; the permit issued the next day. The submission is not in the production.
- **What was discussed with Jack West before October 24, 2019, and at the November 18, 2019 site meeting?** Both are referenced in produced documents. Neither appears in the file.

## Notes on sourcing and limits

- Statutory and code citations above indicate **where the requirement ordinarily comes from**, not a legal conclusion that any provision was violated. The 2017 approval was granted under the **former Borough of Princeton** land use ordinance (§§ 17A-193, 17A-254, 17A-257, 17A-390); the 2019 permit was issued under the **consolidated Princeton Code** (§§ 10B-226, 10B-246.1, 10B-254, 10B-258). Section numbering should be verified against the code in effect on each date before any of this is relied on.
- The identity of the parent sheet for the October 30, 2019 stamped drawing is **not established**. What is established is that the drawing depicts driveway and garage work and does not depict paving of the parking lot.
- No area figure should be attributed to the permit. The 4,400 SF appears on the stamped drawing and also on Sheet 5 of the 2016 set; whether it describes what was authorized in 2019 is unresolved.
- This chronology is built on the municipality's own records, obtained under OPRA, plus recorded instruments. It contains no characterization of any official's motive, competence or intent, and should not acquire one.
